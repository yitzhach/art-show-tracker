# Audit — November 2026

A read-only audit of the tracker, run 2026-11-13 at commit `75ede59`, asking
one question: what stands between a tool built around one artist and a
product other artists can use. `docs/product-plan.md` is what came out of it.

**How it was done.** The code and docs were read. The seven live pages were
opened at phone and desktop size and clicked through in a throwaway browser
(no sign-in). The show sites' own terms pages were opened and read. The test
suites were **not** run by the audit. The planned product and design phases
were compressed into that hands-on pass at the owner's request.

Labels: VERIFIED = read or seen directly. LIKELY = inferred; says what would
confirm it. Severity is given as *today → once opened to others*.

---

## Findings

No Critical findings: no secrets in the repo or its history (pattern scan),
no broken core workflow, no injection bug found.

**Performance is fine, and was measured** on the live site from a fast
connection: the ledger is 337 KB over 34 requests and paints in about half a
second; other pages are 7–50 KB. `fit-data.json` is 1.9 MB on disk and
113 KB over the wire. Not measured: a phone on fairground signal.

**Seen on the live site at phone size:** the show drawer is the strongest
screen in the app — score, honest "estimate" chips, the reasons. Against
that: the catalogue's controls fill two screens before the first show; the
ledger header wraps ten controls over five rows; the header says "237 shows"
and the list says "135 of 240"; Account & sync asks a newcomer for a
Supabase project URL and key.

**Leave alone:** the honesty rules and their tests; the DOM-free maths
modules; the escaping discipline; the network-first service worker; the
same-origin API design; importers that refuse an unknown file whole; CI on
every push; page weight.

## What the show sites allow

Each row is quoted from the site's own page, opened 2026-11-13. This is a
reading of their terms, not legal advice.

| Site | On collecting with a tool | On reuse |
|---|---|---|
| ZAPPlication (Creative West General Terms, which ZAPP's terms include; they also cover CaFÉ) | "You agree not to access (or attempt to access) any of the Services through any automated means or in any other manner other than through the interface that is provided by Creative West." | "…only use the Services and Content … for personal and/or internally related matters and, unless permitted to do so by Specific Terms with Creative West, you will not reproduce, duplicate, copy, sell, trade or resell the Services or Content for any purpose and will not exploit for any commercial purpose any portion…" |
| Juried Art Services | "You may not copy, sell, rent, scrape, crawl, mirror, reverse engineer or build a competing database from the Services…" | same clause |
| Eventeny | "Don't access, search or create accounts for the Services by any means other than our publicly supported interfaces (for example, scraping, spidering or crawling)." | — |
| FestivalNet | "You agree not to scrape, copy or collect data from the Service site without explicit written permissions, and not to use automated browsing tools (bots), either you are logged in or not." | "All material and data on this site is copyrighted." |
| EntryThingy | No clause on automated access found | Its crawler rules allow public pages and block `/api/` |
| ArtCall | Not read: the terms page is behind a bot check | — |

What follows from it:

- Four of the five readable sites forbid collecting with a tool. None offers
  a public feed.
- "Only the key data, not their text" helps with copyright — facts are not
  protected in the US — and not with these terms, which govern how the site
  is reached and commercial use, whatever is taken.
- Creative West's "Specific Terms" wording is an open door to a written
  agreement.
- A person looking at a page and copying a show's facts into their own
  season is ordinary use of the site. That is the path the plan builds on.
